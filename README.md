# 🌐 U.S. Domestic Remedy Exhaustion — Stage 1

📘 Public‑Facing Repository Overview

This repository documents Stage 1: U.S. Domestic Remedy Exhaustion, a structured research and documentation project examining how domestic legal, procedural, and oversight mechanisms function when a U.S. national reports severe harm — including torture‑like conduct — involving federal or municipal actors.

It organizes statutory analysis, jurisdictional mapping, and procedural documentation across:

- ⚖️ federal criminal law  
- 🏛️ judicial‑branch accountability  
- 🚓 municipal policing in Washington, D.C.  
- 📝 mandamus mechanisms  
- 🌍 extraterritorial conduct (e.g., U.S. embassies)  
- ♿ disability‑rights barriers  
- 🛡️ human‑rights‑defender protections  
- 🔍 federal law‑enforcement conflict assessment  

This is a research project, not legal advice.

---

🛑 Why Transnational Repression Requires This Repository

Transnational repression occurs when a state uses:

- judicial actors,  
- administrative systems,  
- policing structures,  
- immigration systems,  
- or diplomatic facilities  

to intimidate, harm, or silence a human‑rights defender across borders.

When the alleged perpetrators are U.S. officials, the situation becomes structurally complex because:

---

🇺🇸 U.S. Nationals Are Not Subject to ICC Jurisdiction

This is a foundational reality.

❌ The United States is not a party to the Rome Statute.

❌ The ICC has no jurisdiction over U.S. nationals for conduct occurring:
- inside the United States,  
- in U.S. facilities abroad (including embassies),  
- or by U.S. officials acting under color of U.S. law.

Therefore:

- ICC cannot prosecute U.S. judges, clerks, police, or embassy personnel.  
- ICC cannot investigate U.S. domestic conduct.  
- ICC cannot intervene in U.S. domestic torture allegations.  

This is not a loophole — it is the legal structure of ICC jurisdiction.

---

🌎 The Inter‑American Court of Human Rights Has No Jurisdiction Over the United States

This is equally important.

❌ The U.S. signed the American Convention on Human Rights

❌ The U.S. never accepted the Court’s contentious jurisdiction.

Therefore:

- The Inter‑American Court cannot hear cases against the U.S.  
- The Inter‑American Court cannot order remedies.  
- The Inter‑American Court cannot investigate U.S. officials.  

This is not a procedural barrier — it is a complete jurisdictional absence.

---

🌐 UN Treaty Bodies Require Domestic Exhaustion

UNCAT, ICCPR, and other treaty mechanisms can review complaints only after domestic remedies are exhausted.

They cannot intervene directly.  
They cannot bypass domestic systems.  
They cannot act while domestic remedies remain available.

🔍 Result: Domestic exhaustion is mandatory.

This repository exists to create a structured, fact‑checked record of:

- domestic mechanisms,  
- nondiscretionary duties,  
- procedural failures,  
- jurisdictional contradictions,  
- and exhaustion points.
